How to get a MiniMax

There are two routes: print it yourself from the official STL package, or buy printed parts from a licensed supplier in your region. Either way it starts with an STL licence from Castlerock Yachtworks.

Route one

Build your own

€25 · MiniMax Builder STL pack v1.0

The official Builder STL package from Castlerock Yachtworks. If you have access to a 3D printer, this is the simplest and lowest-cost way to get started — the hull prints in one piece, standing on its transom, with no supports.

  • Hull and component STL files
  • Sail templates
  • Rig templates
  • Building information
  • Future file updates
  • Entry into the official MiniMax Registry

Route two

Printed kits

Regional · licensed suppliers

There is strong and growing interest from sailors who would rather buy printed parts than print their own. Kits are supplied regionally to keep shipping sane. An STL licence from Castlerock is required and will be confirmed with Brett before printing begins.

  • Printed hull, keel fin & bulb, rudder, rig fittings and stand
  • PLA+ with black TPU for the flexible fittings
  • A-rig and B-rig sail templates (PDF)
  • Colour choices vary by supplier

Two things worth knowing before you order. Printed parts are PLA+ — do not leave a MiniMax in a hot car or in direct sun on a dashboard; suppliers ask you to read their heat-exposure disclaimer. And a finished boat must weigh 120–130 g ready to sail, so watch every gram of paint and glue you add.

The class at a glance

Where MiniMax stands today

What exactly is a MiniMax?

A 220 mm, two-channel radio-controlled one-design yacht designed for FDM 3D printing. The hull prints in one piece on most home printers, the ballast is a cut-down section of a 12 mm stainless bolt from any hardware shop, and the whole boat weighs 120–130 g ready to sail.

Do I need to be an experienced builder to start one?

No — it is designed to be approachable for a first build. Be aware it is closer to building an indoor RC plane than a traditional model yacht: you will use tweezers, and excess weight is the enemy. Small yachts are also genuinely harder to sail well than big ones, so expect a learning curve on the water too.

What electronics do I need?

Two 2-gram servos with 1.0 mm JST connectors (non-negotiable, on weight and fit), a lightweight receiver, and a 3.7 V single-cell LiPo of 120–180 mAh. Around 11 g of radio gear total, and roughly an hour of sailing on a 120 mAh cell. Brett’s tested setup comes from MinimumRC.

Do I need a licence, and what is the Order ID field for?

Yes — an STL licence from Castlerock Yachtworks is required, whether you print your own parts or buy a kit. When you register, you can optionally add the order or licence reference from your purchase. That lets Brett confirm a boat came from a genuine licence rather than a shared file, and verified boats get a green ring on the map instead of the usual orange one. It is optional and never shown publicly.

Are there class rules yet?

A draft (version 1.0) exists and is in the Library above — one-design construction from the official STLs, 90-degree sail-arm sheeting, 120–130 g, single-digit sail numbers 30 mm high. It is not ratified, and one value in it (the centre-of-gravity limit) is still to be set. Brett’s own advice for casual racing is deliberately informal: these boats bounce off each other, so no right-of-way rules.

Is my exact address ever shown on the map?

No. Only the country, region and locality you choose to share are used to place your pin — never a precise address, and never without your consent. The public map data is served through a database view that rounds every coordinate before it leaves the server.

Can I appear in the numbers without a public pin?

Yes. Registering adds you to the fleet count and country stats; a visible pin with your name and photo is a separate opt-in.

How do I update my status later (planning → building → sailing)?

Click Log In, enter the email you registered with, and enter the sign-in code we send you. Your entry loads pre-filled — change the status and save. Registering a second time is never needed and never creates a duplicate boat.

What happens to my data, and can I delete it?

Your email is private — used only to sign you in, never shown publicly or to other sailors. Name, photo and locality appear on the map only if you tick the public-profile box. The STL licence reference is private too, seen only by class admins confirming a genuine licence, and never shown publicly. Log in any time to review, correct or delete your own entry — deleting removes your pin, your details and any verification note immediately, and cannot be undone. If you'd also like your sign-in email removed from the system entirely, contact Castlerock Yachtworks (see Links) and they can do that from the admin side.

Which privacy laws apply here?

Both New Zealand's Privacy Act 2020 (Castlerock Yachtworks is based in Dunedin) and the EU/UK GDPR (many registrants are in the EU) apply, since together they cover where the class is run from and where sailors are registering from. Neither actually requires data to be encrypted in the database — both ask for security "reasonable"/"appropriate" to the risk, not a fixed checklist. This site meets that with access control instead: your email and licence reference are restricted by database rules so only you and a signed-in class admin can ever read them, rather than being encrypted outright — encrypting them would make everyday things like signing in or an admin confirming a licence impractical. Both laws also give you the right to see, correct and delete what's held about you, which is exactly what logging in and using Delete my entry does.

Is this site run by Castlerock Yachtworks?

Not yet — it is a community-built proposal being put to Brett and the group admins. If adopted, ownership and hosting will be arranged with Castlerock directly.

Beyond the map

Suggestions to bring to Brett alongside the fleet map

01

Build logs

A simple photo-dated log per boat — hull, rig, first splash — so new builders can watch a full build unfold instead of only seeing finished photos.

02

Licence verification

The order-reference field is already in the database. A small admin view would let Brett tick off genuine licences and show verified boats distinctly on the chart.

03

Regatta & meetup board

Owners post local sailing dates; the map highlights pins near an upcoming event so nearby sailors can find each other.

04

“Nearby sailors” opt-in

With consent, show owners the closest other MiniMax builders — separate from the public map pin.

05

Sail number registry

Numbers are a single digit 1–9, so they repeat. A per-region register would keep local fleets from fielding three number 4s.

06

Bilingual UI

Given how international the group already is, a lightweight language toggle costs little and widens who registers.

07

Printer profile library

Builders share the slicer settings, filament and layer height that produced a hull inside the weight limit — the fastest way to stop a first print coming out 20 g heavy.

08

Tuning sheets

Per-rig notes: mast rake, jib lead position, sheeting angle and the wind range each setup suits. Small boats reward this more than big ones.

09

Rules feedback thread

The draft rules still have an unset centre-of-gravity limit. One place to collect builder comments would give Brett something to ratify from.
